# prEN 18286 vs ISO/IEC 42001: what you actually need for the EU AI Act

> 🇷🇺 [Читать на русском](./ru/pren-18286-vs-iso-42001.md) · ⬅ [Back to home](./README.md) · 📖 [Standard overview](./README.md)

> **TL;DR.** If your company sells AI products in the EU, ISO/IEC 42001 alone is **not enough** for EU AI Act compliance. A dedicated European harmonised standard — **EN 18286:2026**, «Quality management system for EU AI Act regulatory purposes» — was published on 12 July 2026; **presumption of conformity** under Article 40 of the AI Act follows only once it is cited in the Official Journal (not yet done). ISO/IEC 42001 does **not** grant it. That said, EN 18286's Annex D maps to ISO/IEC 42001 controls — so, by common estimates, an existing 42001 certificate cuts EN 18286 work by 60–70 %.

## 1. What prEN 18286 is

**prEN 18286** — «Artificial intelligence — Quality management system for EU AI Act regulatory purposes» — is a harmonised European standard being developed by CEN-CENELEC Joint Technical Committee 21 (JTC 21) under an official mandate from the European Commission issued in May 2023.

The standard's purpose is to give providers of high-risk AI systems under the EU AI Act a concrete QMS framework whose application yields **legal presumption of conformity** with the regulation.

**Key dates:**
- May 2023 — the European Commission issues Standardisation Request M/593 to CEN-CENELEC covering ten areas of the AI Act.
- October 2025 — JTC 21 publishes the prEN 18286 draft for public enquiry.
- 12 July 2026 — CEN-CENELEC publishes **EN 18286:2026**. Citation in the Official Journal of the EU (which grants presumption of conformity) is still pending.

prEN 18286 was the **first** harmonised standard under the EU AI Act to reach the enquiry stage; in July 2026 it was published as EN 18286:2026.

## 2. Why ISO 42001 does not equal EU AI Act compliance

This is the uncomfortable truth that often gets glossed over in sales conversations.

**What you have been told:** "ISO/IEC 42001 covers 40–50 % of EU AI Act requirements."
**This is true.** But 40–50 % ≠ 100 %.

The official position of the AI Office (European Commission, May 2024):

> ISO/IEC 42001 is not fully aligned with the final AI Act text and is not part of the EU AI Act harmonisation process.

What "not part of the harmonisation process" means:

- ISO/IEC 42001 is an international standard (ISO/IEC) developed by JTC 1/SC 42. It was not developed under a European Commission mandate.
- prEN 18286 is a European standard (CEN-CENELEC) developed under request M/593 specifically for the EU AI Act. Once published in the OJ, it becomes a **harmonised standard** in the sense of Regulation 1025/2012.

Only harmonised standards confer presumption of conformity. ISO/IEC 42001 does not fall into that category and will not in the foreseeable future.

## 3. The legal difference: Article 40 and presumption of conformity

Article 40 of the EU AI Act operates as follows:

> "AI systems or general-purpose AI models which are in conformity with harmonised standards or parts thereof, the references of which have been published in the Official Journal of the European Union [...], shall be presumed to be in conformity with the requirements [of this Chapter] [...] to the extent that those standards cover those requirements."

**In practice:**

| Scenario | Regulatory status |
|----------|-------------------|
| You apply prEN 18286 (after OJ publication) | Conformity is presumed — burden of proof shifts to the regulator |
| You apply ISO/IEC 42001 alone | You must demonstrate conformity yourself — burden stays with you |
| You apply both | Presumption + a stronger risk management posture |

This is not a theoretical distinction. In a national-regulator or notified-body assessment, the first position saves tens of hours of legal work and hundreds of thousands of euros in fine exposure.

## 4. What prEN 18286 covers that ISO 42001 does not

| Area | ISO/IEC 42001 | prEN 18286 |
|------|----------------|------------|
| Managing the AIMS as a system | ✅ Comprehensive | 🟡 Built on 42001 (Annex D) |
| Controls (Annex A vs. prEN clauses) | 38 controls in 9 areas | 8 QMS requirement clauses |
| AI risk management | ✅ Control A.5 + ISO/IEC 23894 | ✅ + high-risk classification specifics |
| Data governance | ✅ Controls A.7 | ✅ + concrete AI Act Article 10 requirements |
| Technical documentation | 🟡 General | ✅ Direct mapping to AI Act Annex IV |
| Record keeping (logging) | 🟡 Control A.6.2.8 | ✅ Direct mapping to AI Act Article 12 |
| Transparency to deployers | 🟡 Control A.8.2 | ✅ Direct mapping to AI Act Article 13 |
| Human oversight | ✅ Three levels | ✅ Direct mapping to AI Act Article 14 |
| Accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity | 🟡 Via A.6.2.4 | ✅ Direct mapping to AI Act Article 15 |
| Post-market monitoring | 🟡 PDCA cycle | ✅ Direct mapping to AI Act Article 72 |
| Conformity assessment | ❌ | ✅ Direct mapping to AI Act Article 43 |
| Registration in the EU AI Office database | ❌ | ✅ Direct mapping to AI Act Article 49 |
| Incident reporting to national regulators | ❌ | ✅ Direct mapping to AI Act Article 73 |

**Bottom line:** ISO/IEC 42001 builds a management system. prEN 18286 builds a management system **plus** concrete procedures aligned directly with specific Articles of the AI Act.

## 5. Annex D: how prEN 18286 builds on ISO 42001

EN 18286:2026 includes Annex D, "Mapping to ISO/IEC 42001:2023 controls" — a mapping between the two, not a statement of requirement equivalence. This allows organisations already certified to 42001 to add what is missing rather than rebuild a QMS from scratch.

Indicative additional work for a 42001-certified organisation:

- **30–40 %** additional documentation (mapping to specific AI Act Articles).
- **20–30 %** additional procedures (conformity assessment, registration, incident reporting).
- **10–15 %** additional artefacts in the technical file (Annex IV-compliant technical documentation).

In budget terms:

| Scenario | Additional budget | Time |
|----------|-------------------|------|
| Already have ISO/IEC 42001, adding prEN 18286 | $30–80 K | 3–5 months |
| Starting from scratch with both | $200–400 K | 12–18 months |
| Starting with prEN 18286 only, no 42001 | $150–300 K, but no international recognition | 9–15 months |

**Strategic takeaway:** ISO/IEC 42001 first, prEN 18286 layered on top — this is the cheapest and fastest path to full EU AI Act compliance.

## 6. Publication timeline and AI Act deadlines

### EU AI Act phased application

- **2 February 2025** — prohibited AI practices (Article 5) become enforceable.
- **2 August 2025** — obligations for GPAI models (Articles 51–55).
- **2 December 2027** — obligations for high-risk systems under Annex III (postponed from 2 August 2026 by the Digital Omnibus).
- **2 August 2028** — obligations for high-risk systems embedded in regulated products (Annex I) (postponed from 2 August 2027 by the Digital Omnibus).

### prEN 18286 timeline

- October 2025 — draft published for enquiry.
- 12 July 2026 — published as EN 18286:2026.
- Pending — citation in the OJ EU, which grants presumption of conformity.

**Window of risk:** EN 18286:2026 is published but **not yet cited in the OJ**, so it does not yet grant presumption of conformity; high-risk Annex III obligations now apply from 2 December 2027 (postponed by the Digital Omnibus). Until the OJ citation you will need to:

1. Demonstrate conformity to a notified body on your own evidence;
2. Use ISO/IEC 42001 plus additional evidence tied to specific AI Act Articles;
3. Or wait for prEN 18286 publication and operate under interim mitigations.

## 7. What to do today: CEO strategy

### If you do **not** sell high-risk AI in the EU

- ISO/IEC 42001 certification covers 90 % of what you need.
- Defer prEN 18286 until a concrete enterprise customer asks for EU AI Act compliance evidence.

### If you sell high-risk AI in the EU

**Step 1 (now).** Certify to ISO/IEC 42001 with a narrow scope. This gives you:
- A baseline AIMS;
- A maturity signal for any enterprise buyer;
- A foundation for prEN 18286.

**Step 2 (2026–2027).** Track the OJ citation of EN 18286:2026. Run a gap analysis against the Annex D mapping.

**Step 3 (post-publication).** Extend the QMS to prEN 18286. Obtain presumption of conformity under the AI Act.

### If you are planning a new AI product for the EU

- Do not wait for prEN 18286. Start ISO/IEC 42001 now.
- Build in future AI Act requirements at design time: Annex IV-style technical documentation, Article 12 logging, Article 13 transparency. This minimises the prEN 18286 upgrade later.

## 8. FAQ

### Can we achieve AI Act compliance without prEN 18286 at all?

Yes. AI Act Article 43 permits alternative conformity routes via notified body conformity assessment. This path is more expensive and slower than presumption of conformity via harmonised standards, but legally available. For most companies, the prEN 18286 route will be simpler and more predictable once the standard reaches the OJ.

### If we hold ISO/IEC 42001, can we market ourselves as "EU AI Act compliant"?

No. The accurate phrasing is "built on ISO/IEC 42001 to streamline future EU AI Act compliance". Claiming AI Act compliance without a harmonised standard or a notified body conformity assessment is a heightened regulatory risk.

### What if the final prEN 18286 differs significantly from the draft?

Structurally unlikely. CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 is finalising the text taking industry comments into account, but the core logic (QMS under the AI Act + Annex D mapped to 42001) is aligned with the AI Office. Specific clause details are more likely to shift than the overall architecture.

### Does prEN 18286 affect companies outside the EU?

Directly — no. Indirectly — yes: a growing share of EU enterprise customers will require suppliers to provide either a certificate against a harmonised standard or evidence of AI Act compliance. If you export AI products into the EU, prEN 18286 becomes critical even without a legal EU presence.
