## What accreditation is and why it matters

**Accreditation ≠ certification.**

- A Certification Body (CB) issues certificates to companies.
- An Accreditation Body (AB) assesses the CB's competence.

A certificate issued by an **accredited** CB has international recognition under IAF MLA. A certificate from a **non-accredited** CB is formally valid but is typically not accepted in enterprise procurement, tenders, or by hyperscalers.

**Key ABs for ISO/IEC 42001:**
- **ANAB** (US) — ANSI National Accreditation Board
- **UKAS** (UK) — United Kingdom Accreditation Service
- **RvA** (Netherlands) — Raad voor Accreditatie
- **DAkkS** (Germany) — Deutsche Akkreditierungsstelle
- **JAS-ANZ** (Australia / New Zealand)
- **IAS** (US) — International Accreditation Service

Accreditation for 42001 has been ramping through 2024–2026. It is the youngest ISO management system standard, and the list grows continually. Use the official ANAB directory and IAF CertSearch for current data.

## How to choose a CB

Seven criteria, in decreasing importance:

### 1. Accreditation under your target market

- Selling in the US → ANAB-accredited CB (Schellman, A-LIGN, SGS, BSI).
- Selling in the EU → UKAS / RvA / DAkkS-accredited (BSI, TÜV, DQS, Bureau Veritas).
- Global sales → CB with triple accreditation (BSI).

### 2. CB's 42001 experience

By early 2026, about ten CBs have meaningful 42001 experience. Ask for the count of 42001 certificates issued, not their general ISO portfolio. A CB saying "we do all ISO" but you'd be their first or second 42001 — that is a risk.

### 3. Industry expertise

If you are FinTech — look for a CB with a financial-sector portfolio. HealthTech — with ISO 13485 + FDA experience. Defense — with experience and clearance.

### 4. Scope flexibility

A flexible CB allows a narrow first certificate and scope expansion at recertification. Rigid CBs demand a broad scope upfront, which is slower and more expensive.

### 5. Lead time

Loaded CBs (BSI, Schellman) may schedule Stage 1 in 3–6 months. Less loaded ones — in 1–2 months. This affects your go-to-market.

### 6. Price

The gap between premium (BSI, DNV) and mid-tier (Coalfire, smaller CBs) can be 2–3×. Cheaper does not mean worse — if accreditation is valid.

### 7. Local presence

A CB with an office in your country or city means lower auditor travel cost, reducing total spend by 10–30 %.

## Contract checklist

Items to verify before signing with a CB:

- [ ] **Accreditation:** specific AB named (ANAB, UKAS, etc.). Verify on the AB's site.
- [ ] **Scope statement:** signed before Stage 1. Changes after Stage 1 may incur extra fees.
- [ ] **Dates:** Stage 1 and Stage 2 dates committed. Reschedule limits — no more than 60 days.
- [ ] **Audit team:** key auditor names disclosed. 42001 experience documented.
- [ ] **Languages:** in which language the audit is conducted and the report is written.
- [ ] **Remote audit:** partial remote allowed? Surveillance often full-remote.
- [ ] **Evidence pack:** format and volume.
- [ ] **Nonconformities:** time to close minor (typically 30–90 days), major procedure.
- [ ] **Surveillance:** Y2 and Y3 volume and price locked in the original contract.
- [ ] **Recertification:** conditions and indicative price in year 3.
- [ ] **Termination:** ability to terminate without losing fees paid.
- [ ] **Confidentiality:** NDA signed, marketing rights limited to your consent.
